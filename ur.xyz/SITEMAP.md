## Research reports

The August–October 2026 research report at /research/2026-q3-report, English-only like /mainnet, linked from a card under the /research intro in every language. It reads intro, timeline, methods, milestones, details. The intro grounds the research in its aim (the best encryption and the best speed for everyone, in everyday use, that makes your internet better), what each meter means in everyday use, the headline figures (multiples) and every entry at a glance. The timeline lays out the 39 entries of connect/20261024-RESEARCH-IMPACT.md, each with a plain headline, one key figure and a marker for its kind, and the report's own text a click away; meters (page load, bulk speed, packet path, memory, recoveries) replay the evidence as the reader goes and are never combined into one score. Then the seven methods (each with an animation), the gigabit-class milestone from adaptive windows, and the details: how far each kind of gain went, the instruments, what is still open, and a glossary. On wide screens a sidebar lists the sections and the meters sit in the other margin beside the timeline; on tablets and phones a bar docks under the nav with a menu of the sections and, in the timeline, the meters. The content is data, in react/src/data/research-report-2026-q3.json: scripts/research-report-import.mjs refreshes the figures quoted from the report and keeps the plain layer, and tests/research-report.test.mjs checks that every figure shown is the report's.

The research methodology at /research/methodology, English-only like the reports, linked from a card right under the report's card on /research in every language. The card's hero is the three research loops' rings (the lab innermost, the open competition, the volunteer field nodes outermost), each turning at its own pace in CSS and still under reduced motion; the same rings head the page. The page, written from the research-loops abstract, reads: the discipline the loops share (a frozen baseline, the instrument's noise measured first, every attempt kept), their two outputs (a better network and a better instrument, with the five stages and their two rails), the agents' roles, each loop with its key figures and public sources, and quarterly publication. Its copy is react/src/data/research-methodology.js; its lab figures are the report's and its competition figures the competition card's (tests/research-methodology.test.mjs).

# 7. Capture the flag

Capture the flag at /ctf, and in Russian, Arabic, Chinese, German and Spanish at /<lang>/ctf: the subnet's per-country reward, ten slots in each of the 249 ISO 3166-1 countries every epoch, five for providers and five for extenders (sn CAPTURETHEFLAG.md; the design is sn's design/ctf/design.html, the build plan design/ctf/SEO.md). Its title leads with plain earning words ("Earn crypto by sharing your internet in 249 countries"), because in search "capture the flag" means hacking contests; the H1 keeps the program's name. The hub reads: the live bar (the epoch and the time to its draw, the slot, what was carried and paid, and the flags held and the countries not fully captured, which filter the grid with a view transition), the visitor's country (from the API's /sn/ctf/here, or picked; the static page shows the biggest open pot, or no country before the first draw), Easy wins (eight big pots with an open slot, drawn by pot size, again on each visit and with Shuffle), the grid of all 249 countries (A–Z, biggest pot or easiest wins; all, big pots or held; find by name or code), how the draw works, questions, and the fine print with the chance to win's definition at #chance-to-win. Next steps open on hover or focus on desktop, and a card opens to full width on a tap on tablets and phones.

Each country has its own page at /ctf/<cc> (/ctf/ke), and in each language at /<lang>/ctf/<cc> (/ru/ctf/ke), titled "Earn crypto in <country>: open slots and pots" where that fits 60 characters: the country's slots, pots and chance to win this epoch, or before the first draw the providers the network's dashboard counted online there; questions in the searchers' own words (make money, passive income, remote work: it is not a job), the other big pots, and the countries before and after it. Its alternate is the country's JSON (/sn/ctf/country/<cc>), and it is a WebPage about its Country, part of the hub's Dataset. A country page with its own data or a local note is indexable. One whose own content reads like another country page's but for the country's name, and which has no local note, is noindex, out of the sitemap and page-dates.json, though still built, linked from the hub and served (sn design/ctf/SEO.md §2.1): before launch that is 109 pages, the 75 inhabited countries the network's dashboard never counted, the 7 territories without people and 27 countries it counted alike. The build decides it from each epoch's snapshot (astro/src/pages/ctf/[cc].astro renders every country page body for react/src/components/pages/ctf/indexing.js), so a page is indexed from the first build whose data or local note sets it apart. The translations say the same in each language: the copy is react/src/i18n/pages/ctf/<lang>.js, key for key with en.js; the countries' names are CLDR's (react/src/i18n/pages/ctf/names/<lang>.js, written by scripts/ctf-names.mjs) with the short name a title uses and "in <country>" as each language says it, written by hand; a page's title leads with the language's earning words within 60 characters, its description comes from its data, and its figures, days and plurals follow the language; the draw is a drawing of lots in every language (жеребьёвка, القرعة, 抽签, Auslosung, sorteo), never a lottery. A translation is indexed exactly as its English page is, so a page and its translations form one hreflang cluster, and the Arabic pages read right to left.

The pages are rendered to static HTML from the epoch's snapshot, react/src/data/ctf-epoch.json, so every figure is in the HTML that search engines and AI crawlers read; only the live bar, the cards, Easy wins and the grid hydrate. After each epoch's census and root, scripts/ctf-import.mjs writes the snapshot from GET https://api.bringyour.com/sn/ctf (sn/api/operator.yml), checking it first; `node scripts/generate-og.mjs --ctf` renders the epoch's social card in each language; then the site is rebuilt and deployed. Until the API answers, from epoch 8, the snapshot is the pre-launch one (`--prelaunch`, from the server's country table): no draw, no pots, and the start at about 18:25 UTC on November 27, 2026; before the first root, and after an epoch without a draw, the pages say nothing of winning by default and show no chance to win. tests/ctf.test.mjs pins the snapshot's invariants and the pages' model. The seo-audit reads what each built country page says of its own country as the build did (without its neighbours, its other big pots and the days it states, its names and code masked): every page must follow the indexing rule, so no two indexable country pages differ by the country's name alone; pages that read alike must be countries whose data is the same, else a page has dropped its figures; it lists the noindex pages, which need data or a local note of their own (react/src/data/ctf-local-notes.js) to be indexed; and without a draw it refuses any default win or chance to win. The flags are flag-icons 7.6.0 (MIT), one file per country under /ctf/flags (scripts/ctf-flags.mjs). The nav's Network menu and the footer's Network column link the hub; llms.txt lists the hub, the API and its OpenAPI document, not the 249 country pages.
